# Design System: Agentic Resilience Workbench

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Evidence Workbench"**

An understated, modern and precise working interface for institutional research. A near-neutral canvas on a faint grid, Geist reading text and compact Geist Mono metadata carry evidence, source references and analyst decisions. Selective glass and one focal surface identify current work without competing with the material being examined.

**Key Characteristics:**

- Near-neutral reading surfaces on a quiet, fading workspace grid.
- Geist text and locally hosted Geist Mono metadata.
- Selective glass, opaque reading objects and hairline separation.
- Analytical meaning conveyed by labels and glyphs as well as tint.
- Four workspace destinations and an inventory that opens in context.

The 2026-10-27 design-system transfer is maintained in code. The [active theme](src/assets/styles/themes/evidence-workbench.css) owns values, the [global stylesheet](src/styles.css) owns shared utility composition, and shared components own reusable variants. The local design-tool export is not a build input or a second maintained authority. [PRODUCT.md](PRODUCT.md) owns product behavior and [context/TECH.md](context/TECH.md) owns enforcement.

Run `pnpm design:sync` to refresh CSS-backed colors, typography and radii in this frontmatter and regenerate `.impeccable/design.json`. `pnpm design:check` detects drift without writing. Component capture values and static HTML/CSS samples require review when their runtime owners change; synchronization does not infer those definitions from React variants. Light and dark values are explicit; derived palette strips are illustrations rather than additional application tokens. Static samples document appearance, not workflow behavior.

## Colors

Ordinary primary actions use **Institutional Ink** (`primary`) and `primary-foreground`. **Research Violet** (`brand-purple`) marks focus, selection, the `//` motif and real running work. Installed screens retain the theme's oklab mix for `accent`; `WorkbenchUiRoot` uses the reference-specific accent values defined in the same theme stylesheet. **Quiet Sage** (`brand-green`) remains a restrained recognition signal. **Approval Teal** (`approved`) is reserved for positive analyst approval, never technical success, source availability or proposed inclusion.

Source-preparation progress bars use Research Violet for completed work, a contrasting neutral for the active segment and a quiet neutral for work remaining. Only the active segment animates while running; pause retains the completed fill. The outer track owns both rounded ends and clips the square internal segment boundaries.

The Sources preparation screen reports what Sources handed to Investigation: documentary needs in rows against the framed questions in columns, each need with its prepared, gap or unfinished outcome and its reason. Mark shape and colour carry the outcome together: a filled violet mark for prepared material, a review-toned ring for a completed search with nothing usable and a dashed ring for unfinished work. Selecting a question brings the needs it relies on forward and recedes the others; the screen never labels a question as covered, because that judgment belongs to Investigation. Dashed vertical rules separate the question columns, and each mark explains the question it serves and its outcome on hover or tap. Long needs clamp to two lines with an explicit Show full need control; the Outcome column holds a collapsible list of each need's documents. How preparation ran stays one disclosure below the needs; a secondary action beside Inspect sources scrolls to it, then opens it with focus. The Sources reader is a notebook inside one viewport: the source list (filter, collapsible Retained and Not retained groups) sits beside the document, which scrolls on its own under a two-row header (title; classification and capture meta, citation stepper, notes control, original link, Full text, Follow up, question chips). The reader opens on the first citation: runs of uncited paragraphs fold into a "paragraphs not cited" control that expands in place and folds again, and Full text restores the whole document. Each cited passage carries a numbered marker in reading order; its note states what the passage contributes, its control and its provenance. Notes have one position per width: from 1280 px always in the margin, whether one note or all are open (shown by default), with the number marker as the toggle; below that an explicit note line under the passage (kind, then Show note or Hide note) opens the note in place and scrolls it into view, and the number is only an index. One control shows or hides every note, and the stepper always opens the focused note. Choosing a question moves to its first citation, labels the stepper with it and states the full question under the chips; a Conflicts filter steps through conflicting citations, and a source list "Go to conflict" link opens a source on its first one. Every Q label explains its full question on hover, focus or tap. The original document opens from an external-link control on the title line, labelled Original when the reader has room. With every note hidden, the text takes the full width. Long active-question text clamps to two lines with More. On narrow screens the source list ends at the viewport and scrolls inside its card. Follow-up requests sit in a Follow up menu in the header. Capture labels say "complete capture" or "partial capture" because a complete capture is not proof of complete reading; never-captured candidates remain open leads, not "excluded". In the source matrix, a faint band marks the Reading column and a slightly stronger one the Contribution columns, the difference in tint separating the two groups without a rule.

Use `foreground` and `muted-foreground` for reading. `surface-glass`, `surface-float` and `surface-bar` distinguish working panels, floating layers and the top bar. `fill-quiet` dresses segmented tracks and icon wells; `divider` separates rows. Shared application fields use `input-surface` with `input-border`. Their fill is slightly darker than cards and popovers in both themes, including grouped controls and selectors. The Continuous thread composer instead uses the selected study's opaque card surface with a transparent text area. At rest it keeps a comfortably padded single input row with the send action alongside; focus expands the input and reveals the action row while preserving multiline drafts on blur. The library's inventory search retains its reference card fill; its field-level focus treatment is described under Components. Shared field controls add the theme-owned `surface-shadow-input` inset and a quieter resting border to distinguish editable wells from outline buttons; focus and invalid-state rings remain explicit.

Evidence classes use `evidence-fact`, `evidence-inference`, `evidence-hypothesis` and `evidence-unknown` with their corresponding `tint-*` fill, label and glyph. Counter-evidence uses rose and its label when an actual opposing relation exists; it is not a fifth mutually exclusive claim class. Source families use neutral metadata so their category cannot imply analytical authority.

`review` is a fill or marker; `review-foreground` is its readable text. Destructive fills retain `destructive`; `destructive-text` supplies readable small text on those tints, including hover. Links use `accent-foreground`, which is violet in light mode and a pale teal in the current dark theme; it is separate from the analyst-approval token. Alpha-valued dark borders and surfaces remain alpha-valued.

OutcomeCard distinguishes reasoned appraisal in violet, limited synthesis in slate blue, justified insufficiency in warm taupe, non-establishment in blue gray, pending judgment in amber, no retained appraisal in desaturated amber, unfinished work in slate and technical interruption in muted red. Its `--outcome-*` colors are scoped to the library in the active theme; they are not global approval or evidence-class tokens.

**The Semantic Color Rule.** Research Violet identifies focus, selection, running work and reasoned-appraisal outcomes; Approval Teal identifies analyst approval, amber identifies pending judgment, and evidence colors identify analytical meaning. Always retain the label and non-color cue.

## Typography

Geist Variable is the shared heading and body family. Geist Mono 400/500 is hosted locally with its OFL license. Consumer-specific landing headline and composer sizing remain local.

- **Display:** responsive page titles, semibold and tightly tracked.
- **Title:** compact section titles.
- **Headline:** screen headline at 40px/44px and medium weight, reduced on narrow screens.
- **Heading:** section heading at 20px and semibold weight.
- **Reading:** the sentence under examination, at 20px and medium weight.
- **Body:** default reading text; body-sm supports compact descriptions.
- **Brief:** long reading text at 15.5px/26px.
- **Figure:** real summary counts, followed by a readable noun.
- **Label:** short uppercase monospaced metadata.
- **Chip:** uppercase monospaced section or integrity disclosure.
- **Meta:** sentence-case monospaced provenance and identifiers.

Shared typography roles are defaults. Authored component measurements remain local: AppraisalBlock, for example, uses a 19px interpretation with a 28px line height.

**The Shared Type Rule.** Use sentence case for controls and prose. Reserve uppercase metadata and monospacing for short structural labels, counts and provenance.

## Layout

`WorkbenchShell` owns outer gutters and the document or bounded viewport layout. Dossier pages declare the bounded mode with `data-workbench-layout="viewport"`; shell styles apply it without child effects changing parent state. `StartResearchPage` owns initial page composition and the creation/confirmation journey; its reading column shares one width across both states, while `ResearchComposerView` owns only input presentation.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:

- Do keep tokens and variants in their runtime owners and regenerate derived documentation.
- Do keep evidence, source membership and analyst judgment visibly distinct.
- Do preserve meaningful source controls, keyboard access, loading geometry and direct links.
- Do inspect both themes and narrow layouts after shared style changes.

### Don't:

- Don't use teal for routine success or evidence colors for unrelated source categories.
- Don't fabricate execution, question relationships, appraisal outcomes or change counters.
- Don't load the local design export at runtime or maintain a parallel palette.
- Don't remove integrity disclosures or substitute a public page for protected workflow verification.
