# Debt Drive: CCC buyout may push interest payments up

CRM Editor November 13, 2026

The buyers of Chicago-based collision repair software supplier CCC Intelligent Solutions are arranging US$2.85 billion in borrowing to help finance a takeover that could push the business’s annual interest bill to approximately US$250 million.

That would mean an increase of more than US$150 million in annual cash interest costs in 2027, according to a Nov. 10 assessment by S&P Global Ratings.

The financing would support the acquisition announced Oct. 15 by a group led by Chicago-based private equity firm GTCR and including Florida-based Elliott Investment Management. Existing CCC debt would also be refinanced.

JPMorgan launched the financing Nov. 9, according to a Nov. 11 report by leveraged-finance news service LCD.

CCC is the supplier behind CCC ONE, software used to write estimates, source parts, receive insurance assignments and manage repairs. The broader CCC network connects more than 35,000 businesses, including insurers, repairers, automakers and parts suppliers.

Under the takeover agreement, shareholders would receive US$7.75 in cash per share. CCC would become privately owned and stop trading on Nasdaq.

The transaction carries an enterprise value of approximately US$6.1 billion, a figure that includes debt and other adjustments. The US$2.85-billion borrowing package forms part of the financing, alongside money from investors.

The arrangement is a leveraged buyout: buyers borrow to help purchase a business, then rely on its future cash flow to support the debt.

The financial burden would extend beyond interest.

S&P analysts forecast that cash remaining after operating expenses and capital investment would fall to between US$35 million and US$45 million in 2027, from approximately US$300 million in 2026. Cash payments for employees’ previously awarded share-based compensation would also contribute to the decline.

The analysts assigned a B-minus credit rating with a stable outlook to Mozart Merger Sub, the acquisition entity initially taking on the borrowing, and its proposed secured term loan.

A B-category rating signals greater vulnerability to difficult business or economic conditions, although the borrower currently has the capacity to meet its obligations.

S&P analysts expect sufficient liquidity to cover the payments and an improvement in financial measures in 2028 as revenue grows and some compensation costs decline.

The borrowing would be supported by a business built largely on recurring subscription fees.

CCC’s quarterly filings show that software subscriptions accounted for approximately 96% of revenue in the three months ended Sept. 30. Revenue reached US$294.1 million, up 10.1% from a year earlier, largely through existing customers upgrading or buying additional products.

Net income was US$30.2 million, compared with a US$2-million loss a year earlier. Debt stood at approximately US$1.28 billion at quarter-end, before the proposed takeover financing.

Githesh Ramamurthy, CCC chairman and chief executive officer, and Tim Welsh, president, have agreed to exchange some of their CCC shares for ownership interests in the buyer’s holding structure.

“With GTCR and Elliott’s experience and resources, we will continue to build on our momentum and extend our leadership,” Ramamurthy said in the acquisition announcement.

Completion is expected in the first quarter of 2027, subject to shareholder approval, regulatory requirements and other closing conditions.
