# CCC Intelligent Solutions Showcase Selection

## Status

Accepted showcase candidate

## Date

2026-07-21

## Candidate Decision

CCC Intelligent Solutions is the primary company for the completed, carefully evaluated showcase dossier.

The selection is intended to demonstrate original technical-economic judgment on a company that is not a recurring named case in Raphaëlle d'Ornano's indexed public research, while investigating an insurance-claims workflow that directly relates to her public work on intent, operational context, workflow intelligence, and economic orchestration.

This decision selects the company and research boundary. It does not predetermine any claim, relationship, dimension position, confidence, direction, or analyst conclusion. CCC must remain configuration or fixture data rather than domain or workflow logic.

## Showcase Scope

The dossier is limited to a photo-led US auto physical damage claim:

```text
Digital first notice of loss and photo intake
→ image validation
→ repairable-versus-total-loss prediction
→ routing under carrier-defined rules
→ estimate generation and review
→ repair-facility orientation and coordination
```

The showcased workflow does not extend to final settlement, payment, or complete claim resolution unless later approved evidence establishes CCC's role in those outcomes. CCC's casualty, medical-claims, subrogation, and broader payment products are outside the primary workflow and may be used only when they provide necessary company-level context.

## Falsifiable Research Question

> Within the public evidence for a photo-led auto physical damage claim, does CCC control a difficult-to-substitute cross-party orchestration point from intake and routing through repair coordination, or does it supply AI and workflow modules that a carrier or core-platform integrator can replace or abstract?

The first interpretation requires evidence of all of the following:

- effective control of routing or cross-party coordination rather than participation alone;
- durable operational context that is material to the workflow and difficult to reconstruct elsewhere;
- action rights or workflow authority that cannot be moved easily to a carrier or another control plane;
- learning or accumulated workflow intelligence beyond executing carrier-owned rules and thresholds.

The interpretation must be weakened or rejected when evidence shows that carriers retain the intent relationship, rules, material context, validation authority, and final decisions, or that CCC's capabilities can be substituted through APIs and integrations without materially disrupting the outcome-producing workflow.

## Why This Case Fits the Candidature

- The workflow is understandable without specialist insurance knowledge: an accident produces photos, a repairability decision, an estimate, and coordination with a repairer.
- CCC connects carriers, appraisers, repair facilities, OEMs, parts suppliers, and other participants, enabling a legible multi-party architecture map.
- Public material supports both a favorable orchestration interpretation and a credible counter-case centered on carrier authority and abstraction risk.
- The case can show the workbench distinguishing network scale, data volume, technical workflow control, and economic orchestration instead of treating them as equivalent.
- The expected demonstration is a contested, inspectable assessment rather than a restatement of vendor messaging or a predetermined positive conclusion.

## Initial Evidence Questions

1. Who receives the claimant's initial intent, and can CCC influence where that intent is structured and routed?
2. Which intake, triage, estimating, review, and repair-coordination events must pass through CCC?
3. Which rules, thresholds, permissions, and approval rights belong to CCC, the carrier, the appraiser, or the repair facility?
4. Which operational context does CCC persist directly, and which data is supplied or controlled by other participants?
5. Can CCC use accumulated claims and repair interactions to improve cross-party coordination, and what public evidence constrains that inference?
6. Where does CCC act directly, where does it recommend, and where must a human or carrier system approve?
7. Could a carrier-owned orchestrator retain CCC as a specialist API while moving routing and outcome control elsewhere?
8. Which network relationships would remain difficult to reproduce if interfaces and data exchange became more standardized?
9. What evidence supports or challenges pricing resilience arising from CCC's position in this workflow?
10. Which critical facts remain unavailable in public sources and must remain `Unknown`?

## Required Showcase Integrity

- Freeze and display a source cutoff date before corpus curation.
- Label the completed dossier as curated or precomputed wherever it appears.
- Keep the genuinely live demonstration separate and stop it at the source-approval checkpoint.
- Preserve counter-evidence and critical unknowns even when they weaken the central hypothesis.
- Include at least one material analyst decision or override with append-only rationale.
- Use only approved claims in the brief and only validated supporting claims in the architecture map.
- Do not equate CCC's network size, historical data volume, AI usage, or technical coordination with economic orchestration without additional evidence.
- Do not present carrier-configured automation as CCC-owned judgment.

## Open Items Before Corpus Curation

- Select and record the exact source cutoff date.
- Define the minimum evidence-question coverage required for the ten assessment dimensions within the bounded workflow.
- Identify independent sources capable of testing CCC's claims about adoption, workflow centrality, and outcomes.
- Determine whether public evidence can distinguish reusable operational context from aggregated historical transaction data.
- Define expected claims, known contradictions, and deliberately unresolved questions only after manual source review.
- Define evaluation thresholds without creating an aggregate resilience score.
