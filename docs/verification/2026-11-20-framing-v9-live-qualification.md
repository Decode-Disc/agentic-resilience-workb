## Confirmed semantic issue in NVIDIA framing

The analyst named the April 2025 restrictions. The supplied 8-K established the license notices and expected charges; it did not establish realized lost demand or competitive substitution. In `nvidia-h20-supplied/agent-saved-framing.json`, question 3 asks about revenue growth “despite the China loss,” while its rationale treats “lost H20 demand” as established. Questions 3 and 6 also attach consequence-bearing conditions to an analyst excerpt that only identifies the restriction. The skill was actually consulted, so resource availability alone did not ensure application of its method.

The requests and actual corrective invocation establish that the first write was refused only because punctuation made the activity excerpt absent verbatim from the scope. The model received precise feedback, repaired that mismatch and successfully saved; it retained the separate semantic problem. See the NVIDIA `requests-1.json`, `invocations-2.json`, `invocations-3.json` and saved framing. This supports a model-authored judgment failure, not another missing-schema-field or lost-feedback defect. `framingProvenance.ts::prepareProvisionalFraming` validates excerpt presence and renders conditional syntax; it does not prove semantic entailment.

The independent report describes both conditions as unsupported premises. The narrower verified conclusion is that hypothetical demand loss is a legitimate investigation topic, but its analyst attribution and the unconditional rationale are unsupported. Conditional wording alone is not an error, and the remedy is not to reject every hypothesis. A corrected program should ask whether and how much demand was lost, then investigate substitution, with system inference distinguished from analyst-established facts. Retain this case as a semantic regression; do not add a keyword guard or more generic instructions as a purported proof of resolution.
