# Worked framing cases

## A new article describes an old event

Observations: on May 12, 2026, an analyst asks about routing decisions in Harbor Systems' logistics platform after a recent announcement. A May 10 trade article describes a January integration. A May 2 issuer release introduces a dispatcher assistant but says dispatchers still approve routes. Both are read; their publication and event dates remain distinct.

Selection: the May 2 release speaks directly to routing authority. The May 10 article supplies integration context, but its publication date cannot make the January event the analyst's intended announcement. Neither passage establishes displacement of human dispatchers.

Resulting program: examine who receives routing intent, owns operational context and authorizes execution; investigate substitution and value capture. Add a conditional question about cross-platform routing if the analyst means the integration. Ask: “Do you mean the dispatcher assistant or the January integration?” Explain that the proposed common program remains useful under either interpretation.

Limits: the assistant's commercial adoption and cross-platform execution rights remain research questions. The event remains open until supported or clarified.

## The analyst's phrase does not establish a workflow change

Observations: an analyst asks whether Vale Studio retains value in review workflows “after the announcement.” A dated earnings release mentions a partnership. An undated product overview describes existing review controls. Neither says the partnership changes those controls.

Selection: read the partnership terms if available because they might distinguish an integration from a commercial referral. Preserve the undated overview as context. Do not attribute “the announcement changes review authority” to the analyst.

Resulting program: retain the requested review activity and ask who controls approval, context and distribution. Investigate whether the partnership changes these roles. If unavailable terms would change source preparation, ask which announcement the analyst means and offer this provisional program.

Limits: a possible workflow change is an investigative question. A conditional branch may examine it without asserting that it occurred.
