# Worked framing cases

## An identified event leaves economic consequences open

Request: on March 18, 2026, an analyst asks whether Larch Industrial can retain its position in factory ordering after its announced withdrawal of the managed-dispatch service. The analyst supplies the March 12 withdrawal notice and names factory ordering as the activity.

Observations: acquire the supplied notice, search for the service withdrawal, and open the result in context. Its displayed passage says the service ends on September 30 and customers must appoint a dispatch operator. The next page says the ordering API remains available; it does not establish who will own customer intent afterward. A separate search result in the captured annual report describes growth in ordering transactions. That result informed the program but was not opened in context or selected as a citation. The withdrawal notice identifies the event; its effects on customer behavior and value capture remain unknown.

Program to save:

- Scope: factory ordering through Larch Industrial after the announced withdrawal of managed dispatch; distinguish ordering access from dispatch execution and customer control.
- Question `ordering`: Who receives ordering intent and owns the operational context after customers appoint a dispatch operator? Rationale: locate the workflow position that could survive withdrawal.
- Question `switching`: What must customers transfer or rebuild to use another ordering platform? Rationale: examine dependencies rather than assuming either retention or displacement.
- Question `economics`: How do transaction growth, dispatch withdrawal and alternative operators affect Larch's ability to capture value? Rationale: test economic consequences without equating transaction volume with durable control.
- Event basis: public, labelled “Withdrawal of managed dispatch,” citing the exact displayed withdrawal passage reference returned by the reader. No conditional questions are needed merely because the economic answer is open.

Observed save result: a revision with the three questions, one event citation and no conditional-question citations. Explain: “The notice establishes the service withdrawal and the continuing ordering API. I saved three questions about workflow position, switching and value capture. The withdrawal passage is cited for the event; the questions do not have individual citation associations. The report's transaction-growth result helped identify an inquiry, but does not establish the answer.” Do not describe all questions as cited or transaction growth as a finding from a contextual reading.

Limit: the notice does not establish future adoption, operator relationships or economic resilience. Those are reasons to investigate, not reasons to leave the named event unresolved.

## A new article describes an old event

Observations: on May 12, 2026, an analyst asks about routing decisions in Harbor Systems' logistics platform after a recent announcement. A May 10 trade article describes a January integration. A May 2 issuer release introduces a dispatcher assistant but says dispatchers still approve routes. Both are read; their publication and event dates remain distinct.

Selection: the May 2 release speaks directly to routing authority. The May 10 article supplies integration context, but its publication date cannot make the January event the analyst's intended announcement. Neither passage establishes displacement of human dispatchers.

Use `acquire_document` with the observed reference to the May 2 release. Search its returned reading reference for dispatch approval with `search_available_material`, then open the match reference with `read_in_context`. Use the displayed passage reference to support the event. If the surrounding discussion continues after a provisional save, use the issued continuation to read the next extent of the same capture.

Resulting program: examine who receives routing intent, owns operational context and authorizes execution; investigate substitution and value capture. Add a conditional question about cross-platform routing if the analyst means the integration. Ask: “Do you mean the dispatcher assistant or the January integration?” Explain that the proposed common program remains useful under either interpretation.

Limits: the assistant's commercial adoption and cross-platform execution rights remain research questions. The event remains open until supported or clarified.

If the saved program contains common questions and a cited conditional question about the integration, describe that association specifically: “The common program applies to either announcement; the integration condition has a recorded reference.” Reading both releases does not make every question individually cited.

## The analyst's phrase does not establish a workflow change

Observations: an analyst asks whether Vale Studio retains value in review workflows “after the announcement.” A dated earnings release mentions a partnership. An undated product overview describes existing review controls. Neither says the partnership changes those controls.

Selection: read the partnership terms if available because they might distinguish an integration from a commercial referral. Preserve the undated overview as context. Do not attribute “the announcement changes review authority” to the analyst.

Resulting program: retain the requested review activity and ask who controls approval, context and distribution. Investigate whether the partnership changes these roles. If unavailable terms would change source preparation, ask which announcement the analyst means and offer this provisional program.

Limits: a possible workflow change is an investigative question. A conditional branch may examine it without asserting that it occurred.
