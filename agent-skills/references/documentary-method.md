# Documentary distinctions

A search candidate is a lead. A retained reading establishes what was visible in that representation. A qualified contribution states the permitted use of an exact passage; it does not establish the truth of every assertion in the document.

Keep document publication, filing and reported event dates distinct. Use explicit origins, preserve conflicts and say when dates are unknown. A recent publication can describe an older event. Retrieval time cannot date an event. Recency does not establish relevance to the analyst's question.

Attribute issuer claims to the issuer. Repetition of the same release by different domains does not add independent confirmation. Independence depends on the producer and underlying reporting, not the hostname. A restriction on one use need not remove another useful contribution.

A partial reading cannot establish absence from the whole document. Distinguish a completed unsuccessful search from an unperformed search, unavailable retrieval, incomplete capture or interrupted preparation. Preserve useful observations and identify exactly what remains unverified.
